## YouTube explainer — 2–4 minutes

Title: Why Your Car Loan Can Be Higher Than the Vehicle Price

Opening:

“If the vehicle price is $27,500 but the amount financed is over $30,000, that does not automatically tell you what went wrong. It tells you which equation needs to be itemized.”

Outline and script:

1. “Start with the selling price after the written discount or rebate treatment.”
2. “Add sales tax, registration, documentation, and other clearly identified charges.”
3. “Add optional products only if you chose them, and request each cash price separately.”
4. “For a trade, compare the current payoff with the written trade allowance. A payoff above the allowance creates negative equity that may be carried into the new balance.”
5. “Subtract cash down, deposits, rebates, and positive trade equity once—never twice.”
6. “Now compare that reconstructed figure with the contract’s amount financed.”

Worked example:

“Selling price: $27,500. Tax and fees: $2,360. Negative equity: $3,000. Cash down: $2,000. The illustrative result is $30,860 financed. Your real contract may use different labels, so ask the dealer to show the same bridge using your written figures.”
