Before you sign a car quote, pause and check six groups of numbers. The monthly payment is important, but it is the result of the deal—not the whole deal.

First, confirm the vehicle. Check the year, make, model, trim, equipment, VIN, and mileage against what you intended to buy. A mismatch here changes every comparison that follows.

Second, find the selling price. Look for the vehicle price, discounts, rebates, and deposits. Keep those lines separate so you can see exactly what changed.

Third, separate the charges. Taxes, title and registration, government charges, and dealer fees may appear under different labels. Ask what each line represents and whether it is included in the amount being financed.

Fourth, review optional products one at a time. GAP, service contracts, maintenance plans, tire and wheel coverage, and accessories should each have a name and price. Do not evaluate a product only by how many dollars it adds to the payment. A small monthly increase can still represent a much larger total price.

Fifth, check the trade and cash lines. Compare the trade allowance with the payoff. If the payoff is higher, that difference may enter the new balance. Confirm cash down, deposits, and rebates are each shown once and applied correctly.

Sixth, read the finance terms. Write down the APR, term, amount financed, payment, finance charge, and total of payments. For example, extending a fictional loan from sixty to seventy-two months can lower the monthly payment while increasing the estimated total interest. Compare the same balance and rate before drawing conclusions.

Now compare every line with the original written quote. Mark what you verified, write down what changed, and ask focused questions about anything unclear.
